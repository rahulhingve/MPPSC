🔴 MUST REMEMBER

Company Rule: lecture uses 1773–1858 as the broad Company-rule phase, with 1858 marking the shift to Crown/“British Raj”.

🔥 HIGH FREQUENCY

1773 = first step of centralization in the lecture; 1833 = final/maximal step of centralization.

🎯 EXAM POINT

1833: Governor-General of Bengal → Governor-General of India; legislative centralization; Company’s commercial role ends.

🧠 MEMORY TRICK

“17-93-13-33 = हर 20 साल की सीढ़ी” → 1773 → 1793 → 1813 → 1833.

2. 🗓 Chronology

1773
Regulating Act, 1773 — lecture treats this as the first step of centralization. Warren Hastings became Governor-General of Bengal; Bombay and Madras were brought progressively into a more centralized framework.
1784
Pitt’s India Act — British governmental control became more direct; Board of Control is emphasized in the lecture as evidence of increasing British Government involvement.
1793
Charter Act, 1793 — comparatively low controversy in the lecture; Company’s charter continued for 20 years; an important sovereignty formulation is highlighted.
1813
Charter Act, 1813 — Company trade monopoly ended generally, but monopoly remained over tea trade and China trade in the lecture; Christian missionaries were permitted; Company financial accounts were more explicitly separated/regulated.
1833
Charter Act, 1833 — maximum/final centralization in the lecture; Governor-General of India created; legislative powers centralized; Company ceased to function as a commercial body.
1853
Charter Act, 1853 — unlike earlier 20-year renewals, no fixed 20-year renewal period was specified; lecture connects this with the beginning of the end of the Company arrangement.
1857
Revolt of 1857 — lecture presents multiple sources of discontent: political expansion, religious sensitivities, military grievances and broader resentment.
1858
Government of India Act, 1858 — East India Company abolished as the governing authority; Crown rule begins.

3. 📜 Act-by-Act Exam Notes

1773 · Regulating Act

FIRST STEP OF CENTRALIZATION
  • Lecture’s central theme: British Government begins controlling the Company through legislation.
  • Governor of Bengal became Governor-General of Bengal.
  • Warren Hastings is the person linked in the lecture with this first-stage centralization.
  • Use as the anchor point for the later 1793 → 1813 → 1833 sequence.
🎥 Source ≈ 06:45

1784 · Pitt’s India Act

BRITISH GOVERNMENT CONTROL
  • Lecture stresses the creation of a Board of Control.
  • Military/diplomatic decisions increasingly came under British governmental oversight.
  • Conceptual takeaway: control shifts from a purely Company-centered arrangement toward stronger Crown/Government supervision.
🎥 Source ≈ 13:18

1793 · Charter Act

20-YEAR RENEWAL
  • Lecture describes the Act as relatively non-controversial.
  • Company charter continued for another 20 years.
  • Lecture highlights: territorial acquisitions by Company servants were treated as being on behalf of the Crown rather than private Company officers.
  • Country trade is mentioned; lecture connects it with the opium trade to China.
🎥 Source ≈ 19:00

1813 · Charter Act

MONOPOLY BREAK
  • Company’s trade monopoly generally ended.
  • Important exception in the lecture: tea trade + China trade.
  • Christian missionaries were permitted to enter India.
  • Company revenue and commercial profits were more clearly separated/accounted for.
Mnemonic: “चा–चा बचा” = चाय + China monopoly survives.
🎥 Source ≈ 29:39

1833 · Charter Act

MAXIMUM CENTRALIZATION
  • Governor-General of India designation.
  • Legislative power concentrated at the central level; provincial legislative arrangements described in the lecture as abolished.
  • Governor-General of India received broad civil + military authority in the lecture’s explanatory framing.
  • East India Company’s role as a commercial body ended; it became an administrative body.
  • Lord William Bentinck = first Governor-General of India.
🎥 Source ≈ 42:00

1853 · Charter Act

NO FIXED 20-YEAR TERM
  • Lecture contrasts it with the earlier 20-year renewal pattern.
  • Company continuation was no longer expressed as another fixed 20-year renewal.
  • Lecture also says the idea of open competitive civil-service recruitment was proposed in 1833 but not implemented then.
⚠️ Distinguish: “proposed in 1833” ≠ “fully implemented in 1833”.
🎥 Source ≈ 52:30

1857 · Revolt

TURNING POINT
  • Lecture links the revolt to multiple forms of discontent, including political and religious/military grievances.
  • The lecture uses the Enfield cartridge/Mangal Pandey episode as a religious-sensitivity example.
  • For exam study, treat 1857 as the direct bridge to 1858 Crown rule.
🎥 Source ≈ 68:20

1858 · Government of India Act

COMPANY RULE ENDS
  • East India Company’s governing role ended.
  • Lecture labels the post-1858 period as British/Crown Rule.
  • Use as the final endpoint of this lecture’s institutional storyline.
🎥 Source ≈ 72:10
